About the IDRS

The Indian Diabetes Risk Score was developed from the Chennai Urban Rural Epidemiology Study (CURES) for use in Indian populations. It uses four items only — age, waist circumference, physical activity and family history of diabetes — and needs no blood test. It estimates risk and suggests whether testing is worthwhile; it cannot tell you whether you have diabetes.

What your score means

Below 30 · Low riskContinue healthy eating and regular activity, and recheck periodically.
30–50 · Moderate riskA fasting blood glucose test is worth doing, along with diet and activity changes.
60 and above · High riskAsk your doctor about HbA1c and fasting glucose testing without delay.

Every item scores in tens: age 0, 20 or 30; waist 0, 10 or 20; physical activity 0, 10, 20 or 30; family history 0, 10 or 20. Totals are therefore always multiples of 10, so scores between 51 and 59 cannot occur.

Frequently asked questions

Why is this score specific to India?

The cut-offs, especially for waist circumference, were derived for Indian populations, who develop metabolic risk at lower measurements than the older international thresholds assumed.

How often should I retake this?

Once a year is usually enough, or sooner if your waist measurement, activity level or family history changes.

Are my answers stored?

They stay in your browser unless you are signed in and choose to save the result to your account.
